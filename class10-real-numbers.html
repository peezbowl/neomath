1. The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic tells us that every composite number can be broken down uniquely into prime numbers, like atomic building blocks:

Theorem Statement

Every composite number can be expressed (factorised) as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique, apart from the order in which the prime factors occur.

In general, for any composite number x:
x = p₁ × p₂ × ... × pₙ where p₁, p₂, ... are primes written in ascending order (p₁ ≤ p₂ ≤ ... ≤ pₙ).

2. Finding HCF and LCM using Prime Factorisation

  • HCF (Highest Common Factor): Product of the smallest power of each common prime factor involved in the numbers.
  • LCM (Least Common Multiple): Product of the greatest power of each prime factor involved in the numbers.
Core CBSE Formula (Valid for Two Positive Integers a and b only)
HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b

⚠️ Warning: This formula does NOT hold true for three numbers (a, b, c)!

3. Revisiting Irrational Numbers (Proof by Contradiction)

A real number is called irrational if it cannot be written in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0. In the Class 10 Board Exam, proving that √2, √3, or √5 is irrational is asked almost every year (3-mark question).

Supporting Theorem (NCERT Theorem 1.3)

Let p be a prime number. If p divides a², then p divides a, where a is a positive integer.

Standard CBSE Board Proof Frequently Asked 3-Mark Question
Theorem: Prove that √3 is an irrational number.
1
Assumption (Method of Contradiction):
Let us assume, to the contrary, that √3 is a rational number.
Therefore, we can find co-prime integers a and b (where b ≠ 0 and HCF(a, b) = 1) such that:
√3 = a / b.
2
Squaring both sides:
b√3 = a
Squaring both sides gives: 3b² = a² ... (Equation 1)
This implies that 3 divides a².
Therefore, by Theorem 1.3, 3 also divides a.
3
Substitute a = 3c:
Since 3 divides a, we can write a = 3c for some integer c.
Substituting into (Equation 1):
3b² = (3c)² = 9c²
Dividing by 3: b² = 3c².
This means that 3 divides b², which implies that 3 divides b.
4
Arrive at Contradiction:
From steps 2 and 3, both a and b have at least 3 as a common factor.
But this contradicts our initial fact that a and b are co-prime (HCF = 1)!
This contradiction arose because of our incorrect assumption that √3 is rational.
Hence, √3 is irrational. (Q.E.D.)

4. Solved Board Exam Questions

Board PYQ Example HCF-LCM Application
Question: Given that HCF(306, 657) = 9, find LCM(306, 657).
1
We know the standard relation:
LCM(a, b) = (a × b) / HCF(a, b)
2
Substitute a = 306, b = 657, and HCF = 9:
LCM(306, 657) = (306 × 657) / 9
= 34 × 657 = 22,338.
Board Short Question (2 Marks) Composite Number Check
Question: Explain why 7 × 11 × 13 + 13 is a composite number.
1
Take out the common factor 13:
7 × 11 × 13 + 13 = 13 × (7 × 11 + 1)
= 13 × (77 + 1) = 13 × 78
= 13 × 13 × 6 = 13² × 2 × 3
2
Since the given expression can be factorised as a product of more than two prime factors (2, 3, and 13), by the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, it is a composite number.

CLASS 10 MATHEMATICS

Build strong foundations. Understand the "why", not just the "how".

CHAPTERS & SYLLABUS

01

Real Numbers

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic and proofs of irrationality

Explore prime factorisation uniqueness, relationship HCF(a,b) × LCM(a,b) = a × b, and rigorous proofs by contradiction for the irrationality of √2, √3, and √5.

Key Concepts: Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (Every composite number can be uniquely factored into primes) • HCF and LCM prime factorisation method and identity: HCF × LCM = a × b • Proof by contradiction that √2, √3, √5 are irrational numbers • Decimal expansions and prime factors of denominator (2^n · 5^m)
02

Polynomials

Zeroes of quadratic polynomials and relationship with coefficients

Geometrical meaning of zeroes (parabolas crossing x-axis), relationship between zeroes and coefficients (sum = -b/a, product = c/a), and forming quadratics.

Key Concepts: Geometric representation: Parabola opens upwards (a > 0) or downwards (a < 0) • Sum of roots: α + β = -b/a • Product of roots: αβ = c/a • Constructing quadratic polynomial: k[x² - (α + β)x + αβ]
03

Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables

Consistent vs inconsistent systems, substitution, elimination, and word problems

Methods to solve systems of linear equations: graphical consistency tests (a1/a2 ratios), substitution method, elimination method, and upstream/downstream speed problems.

Key Concepts: Conditions for consistency: Unique solution (a1/a2 ≠ b1/b2), Infinitely many (a1/a2 = b1/b2 = c1/c2), No solution (a1/a2 = b1/b2 ≠ c1/c2) • Algebraic methods: Substitution and Elimination by equating coefficients • Speed-distance, upstream-downstream boat models, and fraction word problems
04

Quadratic Equations

Factorisation, Quadratic Formula, and nature of roots via Discriminant

Master standard form ax² + bx + c = 0, the discriminant D = b² - 4ac, identifying real/equal/distinct roots, and solving applied quadratic word problems.

Key Concepts: Standard form ax² + bx + c = 0, a ≠ 0 • Solving by factorisation (splitting middle term) • Quadratic formula (Sridharacharya formula): x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a • Nature of roots: D > 0 (two distinct real roots), D = 0 (two equal real roots), D < 0 (no real roots)
05

Arithmetic Progressions (AP)

nth term, common difference, and sum of first n terms

Understand arithmetic sequences with constant common difference d. Compute the nth term a_n = a + (n-1)d and sum of n terms S_n = n/2 [2a + (n-1)d].

Key Concepts: First term a, common difference d (can be positive, negative or zero) • General nth term formula: a_n = a + (n - 1)d • Sum of first n terms: S_n = n/2 [2a + (n - 1)d] or S_n = n/2 [a + l] • Finding the nth term from sum: a_n = S_n - S_{n-1}
06

Triangles (Similarity)

Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales) and AAA, SAS, SSS similarity

Rigorous proof of Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT) and its converse, criteria for similarity of triangles, and solving high-frequency board questions.

Key Concepts: Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales Theorem) proof and applications • Converse of Basic Proportionality Theorem • Criteria for similarity: AAA (or AA), SAS, SSS • Properties of similar triangles applied to shadows and ladder questions
07

Coordinate Geometry

Distance formula, Section formula, and Midpoint coordinates

Calculate Euclidean distance between points in the Cartesian plane, divide segments in given ratios using the Section Formula, and find centroids.

Key Concepts: Distance formula: d = √[(x2 - x1)² + (y2 - y1)²] • Collinearity of three points using distance formula • Internal Section formula: P(x, y) = [(m1*x2 + m2*x1)/(m1+m2), (m1*y2 + m2*y1)/(m1+m2)] • Midpoint formula: M = [(x1 + x2)/2, (y1 + y2)/2]
08

Introduction to Trigonometry

Trigonometric ratios, values at 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°, and identities

Unlock trigonometry: sin, cos, tan, cosec, sec, cot for acute angles in right triangles, standard trigonometric table, and the 3 fundamental Pythagorean identities.

Key Concepts: Trigonometric ratios: sin = P/H, cos = B/H, tan = P/B • Exact values table at 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, and 90° • Pythagorean identities: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ, 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ • Proving trigonometric identity equations
